    More than a game of chance.
    A game of strategy.

    Built around collecting clues, completing memories, and making meaningful decisions.

    Each Memory consists of five Clue Cards, with one clue representing each of the five emotions: Joy, Fear, Disgust, Anger, and Sadness.

    Players move across interconnected paths, collect clues, accumulate Energy, negotiate exchanges, and protect their progress using Memory Shields. As the game progresses, players must decide when to cooperate, when to negotiate, and when to interfere with their opponents.

    Every clue matters. Every exchange has a consequence. And every Memory brings you closer to victory.

    One Memory.
    Five Clues.
    One from each emotion.

    Every path opens a new possibility.

    The board connects exploration, strategic movement, and player interaction. Every intersection offers a chance to change routes and gain Energy.

    Trigger Spaces

    Draw a Trigger Card and resolve the action specified on it.

    Mingle Spaces

    Interact with other players through negotiations, exchanges, and other permitted interactions.

    Question Mark Spaces

    Draw a Question Mark Card and follow its instructions.

    Intersections

    Intersections connect paths and allow players to change routes. Crossing one earns a player one Energy Point.

    Energy & Memory Shields

    Energy is earned by moving through the board and spent to keep your clues safe.

    +1Energy Point per intersection crossed
    3+Clue Cards needed to obtain a Shield
    10Energy Points required and spent for a Shield

    Cards that change the game.

    Two kinds of space put a card in your hand. Here are some of the effects you might draw.

    Trigger Cards

    Land on a Trigger Space, draw a card, and resolve the action it specifies.

    • Your next roll counts double.
    • Discard 1 Clue. Draw 2 from the same emotion; keep 1.
    • Ignore the next question mark card played against you.
    • Protect 1 Clue until your next turn.

    Question Mark Cards

    Land on a Question Mark Space, draw a card, and follow its instructions. These let you interfere with opponents.

    • Choose an opponent. They choose: lose 2 Energy or give you 1 Clue.
    • Choose an opponent. Take 1 Energy from them and give it to another player.
    • Choose an opponent. They place 1 Clue face-up until their next turn.

    Five emotions. One complete memory.

    Every Memory brings together five emotions. Each has its own character, colour, and place in the game.

    One memory, five clues.

    Every Memory is a moment told through all five emotions. Take the Memory “First Day at School”: each emotion holds one clue, and you need all five to complete it.

    Why five emotions?

    Five unrelated Clues do not complete a Memory. Every clue must belong to the same Memory, with exactly one from each emotion.

    That is why a Clue you cannot use may be exactly what another player needs, and why every exchange at a Mingle Space matters.

    Before your first move.

    Setup is short, and the goal is simple.

    Set up

    Each player is represented by a pawn on the board and receives three randomly drawn Clue Cards. Refer to the printed rulebook for any further setup details.

    Win the game

    The winner is the player who completes a Memory by collecting all five Clue Cards belonging to it.

    Get a Shield

    You need at least three Clue Cards and at least 10 Energy Points. The Shield costs 10 Energy Points.

    Every game tells a different story.

    These stages describe the intended flow of play, not separate phases with rule changes.

    Early game

    Explore and collect

    Begin with three random Clues. Explore the board, decide which Memory to pursue, gain Energy, and discover opportunities to exchange resources.

    Mid game

    Negotiate and protect

    Identify the Memories you are closest to completing. Negotiate for missing Clues, accumulate Energy, and consider obtaining a Memory Shield.

    Late game

    Protect and interfere

    Protect valuable Clues, negotiate carefully, and use available Question Mark Cards strategically to secure the Clues needed to finish your Memory.
